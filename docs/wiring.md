## Pin map (ESP32-S3-Zero)

| ESP32-S3-Zero pin | Goes to |
|---|---|
| 5V | S09 5 V output |
| 3V3 | BH1750 VCC |
| GND | every GND |
| GPIO1 | I2C SDA: BH1750 SDA, ENS160+AHT21 SDA |
| GPIO2 | I2C SCL: BH1750 SCL, ENS160+AHT21 SCL |
| GPIO4 | UART TX → LD2410B RX |
| GPIO5 | UART RX ← LD2410B TX |
| GPIO6 | HC-SR602 OUT |
| GPIO21 | on-board WS2812 LED, nothing to connect |

Also from the S09 5 V output:

| Module | Pin |
|---|---|
| LD2410B | VCC (5 V only) |
| ENS160+AHT21 | VIN. Its 3V3 pin is the output of the on-board regulator, leave it unconnected. ADD, CS and INT stay unconnected too. |
| HC-SR602 | + |

## Power

The S09 has an EN (enable) pin and only runs while EN is high. Bridge EN to its VIN+ pad with a short wire or a solder blob.

```
12 V ──► S09 (EN─VIN+) ── OUT 5 V ─┬─► ESP32-S3-Zero 5V pin
               (220 µF on OUT)      ├─► LD2410B VCC
                                    ├─► ENS160+AHT21 VIN
                                    └─► HC-SR602 +
GND common to everything
```

The only extra part is the 220 µF capacitor, soldered straight onto the S09 output pads.

Notes:

- Never plug USB-C into the ESP while the sensor is on 12 V. The ESP32-S3-Zero 5V pin is tied straight to USB VBUS, so the computer's USB port and the S09 would end up driving the same 5 V line. Do the first flash over USB before you connect the 5 V wire; after that, updates go over Wi-Fi (inside the housing the USB-C port isn't reachable anyway). If you ever need USB again, disconnect 12 V or the ESP 5V wire first.
- Don't use a linear regulator from 12 V. A 7805 or AMS1117 dropping 12 V to 5 V at ~0.3 A burns about 2 W inside a closed 80 mm box, which ruins the temperature reading.
- The radar wants 5 V. Some listings say "5-12 V"; the LD2410B datasheet says 5 V and a supply that can deliver more than 200 mA. Its UART is 3.3 V logic, so it connects to the ESP directly.
- Put 220 µF on the S09 output. Wi-Fi transmit peaks can reach ~0.5 A and the S09 is rated 0.6 A; the capacitor covers the current spikes.
- Measure PIR OUT before connecting it to GPIO6. OUT only goes high on motion, so wave a hand in front of the lens while you measure. The HC-SR602 has its own regulator and normally outputs ~3.3 V even when fed 5 V, but clones vary. If you read more than 3.4 V, power the PIR from the ESP 3V3 pin instead. The ESP32-S3 is not 5 V tolerant.
- Check the I2C pull-ups on the ENS160 board too. With only VIN (5 V) and GND connected, measure SDA and SCL against GND. They should sit at about 3.3 V (the board's own regulator). If they read 5 V, the pull-ups go to VIN and would put 5 V on GPIO1/2; feed that board from the ESP 3V3 pin instead.
- Mini PIRs can pick up 2.4 GHz noise from the ESP's own Wi-Fi and fire on their own. If `PIR motion` triggers in an empty room, first uncomment `output_power: 8.5dB` in the YAML. If that isn't enough, add the RC filter from the optional parts below.
- The I2C modules already have pull-up resistors. Addresses: BH1750 `0x23`, AHT21 `0x38`, ENS160 `0x53`. If the ENS160 doesn't show up, check the I2C scan in the logs; some boards use `0x52`.

## Wires and soldering

- Stranded AWG 26-28, about 8-10 cm per module. Silicone wire bends better around the seats.
- The LD2410B has 1.27 mm pads. Solder thin wires directly; don't try to fit a header.
- If you do use pin headers on the other boards, insert them from the top (plastic above the board) and trim the pin ends underneath. Every seat leaves 2-3 mm of space below the board for pin stubs and solder.
- GND goes to six modules, 5 V to four, SDA and SCL to two. Instead of splicing wires in the air, use a small piece of stripboard (2.54 mm, about 5 strips × 8 holes): one copper strip per net (5V, GND, SDA, SCL, 3V3), each module's wire soldered into its strip. Check the strips for shorts with a meter before connecting the modules.
- Bring 12 V in through a Wago/JST connector and tie the cable for strain relief: while you twist the housing onto the base, it hangs on that cable.

## Optional parts

Not needed for the sensor to work; add them if you want the extra protection:

| Part | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Schottky diode 1N5819 / SS14 (any 1N581x, SS14-SS34 works; a 1N4001-4007 too, the ESP then gets ~4.2 V, still fine) | between the S09 5 V and the ESP 5V pin, stripe towards the ESP | Lets you plug in USB-C while 12 V is connected, without the two 5 V sources meeting. Radar, ENS160 and PIR stay on the 5 V before the diode. |
| 100 Ω resistor + 47 µF capacitor | 100 Ω in series with the PIR +, 47 µF from PIR + to GND | RC filter against Wi-Fi noise on the PIR supply, if `output_power` alone doesn't stop false triggers. |

Estimated current (from datasheets, not measured): ~0.3 A at 5 V with peaks around 0.5 A during Wi-Fi transmit, which is ~0.15 A or 1.5-2 W from 12 V.
